About the data: how records are made, and how much to trust them

Three kinds of record. Regulator rows come from HHS, state attorney-general lists and the HIBP catalogue by fixed mapping, no model involved: they say who, when and how many, and almost never how. Extracted rows come from an 8-K, a news article, a notification letter or a catalogue description read by a language model that fills a fixed schema. Imported rows come from VCDB, re-coded from its VERIS fields. Every row shows its origin tag and its sources.

What the model may and may not do. It may set a mechanism only with a verbatim quote, and the pipeline checks the quote is in the text; a quote that is not found drops the confidence, a mechanism without one is reset to unknown. A count is kept only if the figure appears in the text. A CVE, an actor name or an ATT&CK technique is kept only if the text carries it. Leak-site claims never become records on their own; they wait privately until a filing or article confirms the incident. "unknown" is the default and the most common value, because most sources say nothing about how.

How good is the model. A 20-document eval with human-checked labels (October 2026; small, so read the gaps, not the decimals):

The model's own "unknown" rate on that set was 33% against 15% for the human labels: it is more cautious than a person, which is the direction we want. On eight California notification letters, gpt-5.4-mini needed repair rounds on five and named a forensic firm as the breached vendor; gpt-6-luna needed none and agreed with it on every date. Full tables and the labelled documents are in the repository under eval/.

Known gaps. Coverage follows the sources, so US healthcare and California filers are over-represented and most of the world is missing. Regulator rows carry no mechanism until their letter is read. Organization identity is a name match plus a short alias list, so a subsidiary can appear separately from its parent. Share-price and KEV context is computed at build time from public APIs and is context, not causation. Records are unreviewed unless marked; corrections are logged in docs/CHANGELOG.md and anyone can open an issue.

Glossary: what the values mean

Sources: SEC EDGAR full-text search, the HHS OCR breach portal, Maine and California Attorney General breach reports, the Have I Been Pwned breach list, security news feeds, Mastodon #databreach, the VERIS Community Database (CC BY-SA 4.0), and leak-site claims aggregated by Ransomware.live. Context joined at build time: CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog (via NVD) and daily closing prices from Nasdaq's data API, with Alpha Vantage for older filings. Claims are shown only as a tag on records that another source confirmed; a claim on its own is never published here.

Records marked unreviewed were produced by a fixed mapping or a language model and have not been checked by a person. Every stated mechanism carries a verbatim quote from its source; if the quote is not in the source, confidence is low. The data cannot tell a second breach at the same organization from late coverage of the first; two records with one name may be either.
